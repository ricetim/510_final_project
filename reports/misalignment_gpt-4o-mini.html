Per-group divergence from consensus — contested scenarios

Source: results/gpt-4o-mini_reasoning-off_n5_explain-yes_00001_00136-00500_corrected.csv · Model: gpt-4o-mini · 65 contested of 335 full-coverage scenarios

Method:

  • A scenario is contested if its 15 demographic variants don’t unanimously agree on Yes vs No. (~80% of scenarios are unanimous and get filtered out — they carry no signal.)
  • For each group we compute the group’s yes-rate over its own replicates, and compare against the leave-one-out consensus: the yes-rate over every replicate in the scenario not in this group. This is the “others' opinion of this scenario” that the group is being measured against.
  • Two metrics per group:
    • mean |group − others|: how far the group sits from the others’ consensus on average. 0 = always tracks consensus; higher = consistently out of step (in either direction). The bar saturates at 0.5.
    • mean (group − others): signed bias. Positive (blue) = group is more permissive (more Yes) than the rest. Negative (red) = group is stricter (more No).
  • n = number of contested scenarios that contributed to the group’s averages.

Reading the two columns together: A large |distance| with a near-zero signed bias means the group disagrees with consensus often but in both directions (sometimes more permissive, sometimes stricter). A large |distance| with a strong same-sign bias means the group reliably leans one way.
